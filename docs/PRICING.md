# What to charge, and what must never be charged for

**Nothing here is built.** This is a proposal to be decided on before any
billing code exists, because the shape of the plans decides the shape of the
code and rewriting a paywall after people are behind it is how a product loses
the customers it already has.

## The rule that comes before the price

> **Nothing that keeps somebody safe is ever behind a payment.**

Being admitted at the gate, signing an induction, reading a permit, reporting
an incident or a near miss, seeing a violation written against you, checking
in and out. All free, on every plan, for ever, including when a subscription
has lapsed.

This is not generosity. A worker who cannot report a near miss because their
foreman's card expired is a worker who does not report it, and the register
that was supposed to prove the site was run properly has a hole in it on
exactly the day somebody will ask. It is also the difference between a tool a
safety officer will stand behind and one they will not.

The corollary: **a worker never pays and is never counted as a seat.** The firm
that engaged them pays. A labourer who installs this to see their own hours and
their own tickets is a user, not a line item.

## What the numbers should be

The starting suggestion was $4/month for pro and $70 for lifetime. Both are
worth reconsidering, and here is why.

**$4/month reads as a consumer app.** A contractor deciding whether to keep
their site diary, their permits and their concrete tickets in something is
deciding whether it will still exist in three years. A price that could not
possibly fund that answers the question for them. The tools this competes with
in the small-trade market — Tradify, ServiceM8, Jobber — charge $30–100 a
month, and the site-management ones charge in the hundreds. Being the cheapest
by an order of magnitude is not a position of strength here.

**Lifetime at $70 is a liability, not a sale.** It is about seventeen months of
the subscription, and it buys an obligation with no end: the server is coming
(`docs/SERVER.md`), storage grows with every photograph and video, and the
stores take 15–30% off the top. A hundred lifetime customers is a hundred
people whose costs rise every year against a payment that already happened.
Products die of this.

### The proposal

| | Free | Pro | Site |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price | — | ₪49/month or ₪490/year | ₪149/month or ₪1,490/year |
| People | up to 3 | up to 15 | unlimited |
| Everything safety | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| All five lenses, all registers | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| More than one company on a device | — | ✓ | ✓ |
| Subcontractor chains, contracts, payment applications | — | — | ✓ |
| Signed audit export | — | ✓ | ✓ |

Priced per **company**, not per person, because that is how the money actually
works on a site: the boss pays and the crew does not.

The annual plan is two months free. It is the honest version of what a lifetime
purchase is really selling — the feeling of having paid and being done — without
the perpetual obligation.

**Free has to be genuinely useful, not crippled.** A sole trader and a two-man
crew never pay, and everything works: the catalogue, the registers, the
photographs, the audit trail. They are not a lost sale. They are how the third
person who joins the firm finds out the app exists.

### Israel

- Show prices in ₪ including מע"מ (18%), which is what a consumer price must
  be, and quote ex-VAT alongside for businesses.
- Contractors need a חשבונית מס with the ח.פ. on it. Neither store issues one
  in the firm's name, which is a real reason to offer direct billing for the
  Site plan rather than routing everything through in-app purchase.
- Both stores require that an in-app purchase be available in-app. Direct
  billing can exist beside it; it cannot replace it for consumer sales.

## What has to be decided before any code

1. The numbers above, or different ones.
2. Whether the Site plan is sold through the stores at all, or direct.
3. What happens when a subscription lapses: the position taken here is that
   the data stays readable and exportable for ever and only writing is
   limited, because a firm that cannot get its own site diary out of an app is
   a firm that will never put one in.
4. Whether there is a trial, and whether it needs a card.

Until those are answered there is nothing to build, and building a paywall
against a guess is worse than having none.

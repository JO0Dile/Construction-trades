# Terms of use

**Trades Work Manager — Israel edition**

Last revised: 6 September 2026. Version 1, draft, pending legal review.

## What this software is

A record-keeping tool for construction work: stock, tasks, people, evidence
and money. It runs on the device and holds its data there.

## What it is not

**It is not a safety system, and it is not professional advice.**

The app carries checklists, permit forms, inspection registers and references
to Israeli standards. These are prompts for a competent person to work
through. They do not make a job safe, they do not certify anybody, and
following them is not a defence. Scaffolding is signed off by somebody
qualified to sign it off; an excavation is inspected by somebody competent to
inspect it. The app records that this happened. It cannot do it.

Where the app names a standard or a regulation, the name is a pointer for the
site file. It is not a reproduction of the standard and it is not a
substitute for reading it.

**Being verified in the app is not being licensed.** Recording a contractor
registration number marks a firm as having supplied one. Whether that firm is
lawfully entitled to carry out particular work is a matter for the regulator.

## Money

The app calculates retention, payment terms, overtime bands, VAT and
valuations from figures that are entered into it. The arithmetic is tested;
the figures are the user's. It is not accounting software, it does not file
anything with anybody, and it does not replace an accountant.

## Your data, your responsibility

The data lives on the device. That means:

* **Back it up.** A lost or broken phone is lost data. The export exists for
  this reason.
* **Lock it.** The passcode and the device lock are what stand between the
  contents and whoever picks the phone up.
* **You are the one answerable** for the personal data of the people recorded
  in it — their names, ID numbers, photographs and hours.

## Availability

The software is supplied as it is. There is no uptime commitment, because
there is no service to be up: it runs on the device, offline, and continues to
run whether or not anybody maintains it.

To the extent Israeli law permits, the publisher is not liable for loss
arising from use of the software, including lost records, lost profit, or a
decision taken on the strength of something the app displayed. Nothing here
limits liability that cannot lawfully be limited.

## Governing law

Israeli law, and the courts of Israel.
